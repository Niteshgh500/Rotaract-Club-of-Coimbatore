About Us

We're a club of students and young professionals who believe service works better when it's done together. Since our charter, we've run health camps, taught in under-resourced schools, planted forests, and built friendships that outlast any single project. Every member brings a different skill — we just point them at the same problems.

The name, explained
Ro·ta·ract Rotary + Interact — a Rotary-sponsored club for people aged 18–30, built on leadership, fellowship, and hands-on service.

A short history

Rotaract began in 1968 in North Carolina, USA, when Rotary International chartered the first club to give young adults their own space to lead service projects rather than just observe them. The idea spread fast: within a decade, clubs existed on every inhabited continent. Today there are Rotaract clubs in over 180 countries, each self-governed, each shaped by whatever their community needs most.

Our Story, in Full

What started as a handful of college friends volunteering on weekends grew into a chartered club with its own board, budget, and calendar of projects. We work across four focus areas — health, education, environment, and community development — and we run on the same principle every year: identify a real gap, then close it, together. New members don't wait for seniority to lead something; the newest inductee can chair a project in their very first term.

Rotaract: Meaning & History

Meaning: "Rotaract" blends Rotary and Interact, signalling a club for young adults sponsored by a local Rotary club, but run entirely by its own members.

History: The first Rotaract club was chartered in 1968 in Charlotte, North Carolina. Rotary International had noticed that its youth wing, Interact, had members ageing out with nowhere to continue — Rotaract gave them that home. Growth was steady through the 1970s and '80s as clubs formed across Asia, Europe, and Latin America. In 2020, Rotary International formally lifted the age cap that once ended membership at 30 in many regions, letting clubs set their own upper limit. Today, Rotaract counts well over 200,000 members worldwide, working alongside — but independently from — their sponsoring Rotary clubs.
